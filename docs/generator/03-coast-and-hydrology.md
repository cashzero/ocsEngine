# M6.3 — Coast and hydrology

The sea is a generation option (`sea`: `NONE`, `NORTH`, `WEST`, `EAST`), independent of the style. A sea uses a low-frequency shoreline varying along its map edge, producing one connected sea rather than water speckles or inland lakes; its accepted water range is 6–35%, and a map without a sea may hold at most 2% water. When the options name no sea, a style keeps its own: `WEST` for Mediterranean coast, `NONE` for the others, so those maps (and their IDs) are unchanged. A sea that is not the style's own adds `:sea-<edge>` to the map ID.

New Game offers the sea as **No sea** or **Along the flank** (the north edge: both sides advance west–east along the coast; the Mediterranean coast's own sea in New Game). Who uses it is set per side, independent of the engagement (user, 2026-10-13, replacing the earlier "Behind the attacker" placement, whose BTR-like rules did not need its west/east map layout): **Allied/Axis Sea Cap** (0 = no Sea Cap) and **Allied/Axis inland entry hexes** (yes / no: ports only). A side without inland entry needs Sea Cap of at least 1 (`SEA_CAP_REQUIRED`) and a sea (`INLAND_ENTRY_REQUIRED`). The map generator's `landingEdges` option (west for Allied, east for Axis) then gives that side a port within `landingPortDepth` columns of its edge (a fifth of the width, at least 3): the best coastal place there, nearest the edge, or else a new port town on the shore nearest the edge, on open or wooded ground first, rough or marsh next, mountain last; validation fails the map with `LANDING_PORT_MISSING` otherwise, and the map ID gains `:landing-<edge>`. A 270-request sweep (four sizes, three styles, three engagements, every no-inland-entry combination, 2026-10-13) generated every map with the landing port held by its side. With the sea on the north edge the open corridor that joins the two entry edges runs at 60% of the map height, inside the land.

Ports (19.0) are a fixed map feature (`FACILITY` `facility:port`), not pieces. A sea gets one port per ten coastal land Hexes, at most four, at least two on a flank sea (so the coast on both sides of the front has one) and one otherwise. Ports go to coastal Major Cities, then towns, then villages, spread along the coast (the first nearest the map's middle, each later one farthest from those chosen, preferring six Hexes apart); when the coast has too few places a coastal open or wooded Hex becomes a town with the port, so roads and railways reach it. Validation requires every port to be a coastal settlement on the road network, at least one port on a map with a sea and none without.

Each port has a printed capacity (19.0a) by the place it is in: a Major City 4 SP, a town 2 SP, a village 1 SP (`PORT_CAPACITY_TOKENS`, in Tokens).

## Ports in play (19.0)

A port is a map feature, not a piece; what changes in play lives in its map feature state: who holds it (`controllerId`), its printed capacity in Tokens (`level`) and its hits (`damage`). Each port starts held by the side whose deployment zone it lies in.

- **Capacity (19.0a):** the SP or RE a port loads plus unloads in a phase, 1T of capacity per Token or quarter RE. A port holding an enemy combat unit or in an enemy ZOC (never negated for shipping) is shut. A single combat unit larger than an unused port may use its whole capacity.
- **Damage (19.0b-d):** an air barrage against an enemy port on the Facility Table gives it the numbered result as hits (at most 4). The side holding a port may inflict 1 hit a turn on it with 1 RE of Combat Mode units in the port that do not move, or an Engineer-Capable unit (19.0c), and an Engineer-Capable unit repairs 1 hit a turn for 1 SP (19.0d). Hits leave 80%, 60%, 40% and 20% of the printed capacity after 1-4 hits, rounded to the nearest Token (Port Damage table beside the Barrage vs Facility Table), so a 2 SP port keeps 2T even with four hits.
- **Capture (9.14h, 4.4c):** a port is a non-combat unit of whoever holds it: only an Attack-Capable unit may enter an enemy port, and entering captures it (moving, retreating or advancing).
- **Supply (19.0e):** every port is declared a Supply Source; it serves only the side holding it, while it has 1 SP of capacity and no enemy combat unit or un-negated enemy ZOC is in it. A side with Sea Cap 0 cannot use ports: when only one side has Sea Cap the declaration names that side, so the other, capturing one of those ports, denies it but cannot draw from it; when both have Sea Cap either draws from the ports it holds; when neither has, no port is a source. Port sources also start rail cargo paths, as map-edge sources do.
- **Shipping (19.0f):** Sea Cap points per friendly Movement Phase ship Move Mode combat units that have not moved, Transport Points (their points; loads ride free), Repls (a quarter RE each) and SP between friendly ports, paying Sea Cap and both ports' capacity. Shipped units do not move afterwards and use no other transport that phase.
- **No inland entry hexes:** that side has no land entry edge. Its map-edge entries and edge sources are not declared, its ports are its only Supply Sources (it starts holding at least the one nearest its entry anchor), and everything it receives except aircraft (units, Supply Table SP, Repls, returning units) waits in its reinforcement Box until shipped to a port it holds; only the unloading port's capacity counts. Its default Sea Cap is 0.6 per division; a side with inland entry defaults to 0.25 per division for port-to-port shipping. Both sides may lack inland entry.

Calibration observed in bot-against-bot games (2026-10-10, 32×24, Allied attack, sea behind Allied on the west edge — the earlier layout, 3 ports of 1, 1 and 2 SP): the three ports land 16T a turn against about 36T of Supply Table income, so port capacity, not the 5-point Sea Cap, is the binding limit and SP pile up in the Box (56–86T by turn 4–5). This matches the requested "port capacity and shipping are the main limit" but has not been tuned; the capacities and the Sea Cap default are the knobs.
